What Is an AI Agent?

A brain that knows a recipe.

An artificial intelligence (AI) model can explain how to cook, but cannot prepare the meal on its own.

A brain with hands.

An agent plans, calls tools, checks results and decides what to do next.

A model can tell you how to cook. An agent can use tools to get the cooking done.

Transact

Micropayments — Pay as Agents Consume

Usage-based access

Buy an API response, data point or burst of compute. Avoid a monthly subscription for a five-minute task.

Economics still matter

Fixed fees can make tiny transactions uneconomic. Reach and payment granularity are different capabilities.

Neither interoperability nor micropayments guarantees zero fees, unlimited throughput or instant final settlement.

Part 2 / Interoperable Payments

Avoiding Agentic Walled Gardens

Siloed

Wallet A → Wallet A merchants only

Payment compatibility limits service choice, even when a better service exists elsewhere.

Connected

Agent → supported providers → seller

Separate the agent platform, funding provider and merchant's receiving network.

Shared interfaces can reduce bespoke integrations for every buyer-seller pair.

Compete on service quality, not payment lock-in. Standards still need participating providers and compatible implementations.

Part 3 / Industry Progress and Gaps

Stripe — Wallet Access, Merchant Integration and Machine Payments

What Stripe provides

  • Link: user-granted wallet access via OAuth authorisation; approved requests release a one-time card or Shared Payment Token.
  • Issuing: custom wallets, card controls and monitoring.
  • Commerce Suite: catalogues, checkout, payments and order events.
  • Stripe + Tempo: Machine Payments Protocol (MPP); Stripe also supports x402 for HTTP-based payments.

What that does not prove

  • The cited Link launch requires review of every spend request; more autonomy is planned there.
  • Scoped credentials do not prove the agent chose the right item.
  • Availability varies by integration and country.
  • Merchants retain fulfilment, refunds and disputes.

Addresses credential exposure and integration work, not universal acceptance or purchase correctness.

Sources: Link + Issuing · Commerce Suite · MPP · Availability

Part 3 / Industry Progress and Gaps

Visa — Payment Credentials, Controls and Agent Recognition

Documented approach

Tokenised AI-ready cards, spending conditions and transaction signals. Cloudflare collaboration adds signed agent intent, consumer recognition and optional payment information.

Boundary

A signature does not prove purchase suitability or delivery. The protocol announcement scopes its initial phase to Visa; alignment does not prove universal acceptance.

Sources: Intelligent Commerce · Trusted Agent Protocol

Part 3 / Industry Progress and Gaps

Mastercard — Agent Pay and Machine-Driven Transactions

Agent Pay

Trusted agent participation, agentic payment tokens and registration.

Agent Pay for Machines

The 2026 announcement describes credentialing, enforced permissions, spending limits and high-frequency, low-value transactions.

Multi-rail ambition

Describes settlement across cards, accounts and stablecoins. Partners validate use cases and common rules.

Deployment evidence needed

Verify availability, actual fees, latency and settlement. Headline performance claims are not workload benchmarks.

A participating network can coordinate identity, permissions and payments. That does not establish universal reach.

Sources: Agent Pay · Agent Pay for Machines

Part 3 / Industry Progress and Gaps

The Gaps — A Payment Credential Is Not a Complete Commerce System

Reach

Do actual agents, wallets, currencies and rails connect? Protocols do not create liquidity.

Portable authority

Carry and revoke conditions, not just an amount limit.

Commerce integration

Standardise inventory, shipping, service delivery and refunds.

Economics

Measure full-path fees, conversion, settlement and compliance costs.

Accountability

Resolve wrong purchases, duplicate charges and partial delivery.

Governance

Check market availability, participation rules and switching costs.

A token limit cannot express “buy refundable travel arriving before noon.” Demonstrate end-to-end behaviour.

Part 3 / Industry Progress and Gaps

What Would Convincing Interoperability Look Like?

A real route

The actual funding source reaches the seller in the required market.

Enforced authority

Merchant, amount, currency and conditions survive the route, including pre-execution revocation.

Visible total cost

Quotes include fees, conversion and pending commitments.

Recoverable failure

Retries and timeouts do not duplicate payments or hide the outcome.

Auditable outcome

Trace payment, delivery, refund and dispute across participants.

Real provider choice

Switch providers without redesigning the purchase workflow.

Compare workflows, constraints and evidence, not claims of market leadership.

Conclusion

Complete the Task, Not Just the Payment

Agents create demand

Software discovers, evaluates and purchases on the user's behalf.

Interoperability expands reach

Connected providers let the agent buy beyond one financial ecosystem.

Reliability earns trust

Authority, delivery checks and recovery preserve the user's intent.

The engineering challenge is to complete the whole task reliably, not merely to move money.
